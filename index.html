Muhammad Atif Butt

I am a pre-doctoral researcher in the Learning and Machine Perception (LAMP) team at the Computer Vision Center in Barcelona, advised by Dr. Joost van de Weijer.

My doctoral research focuses on learning and controlling visual attributes in text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models. This includes learning exact colors as prompt tokens in ColorPeel (ECCV 2024), evaluating the color understanding of T2I models in GenColorBench (CVPR 2026), and enabling numeric color control from hex and RGB codes in NumColor (ECCV 2026). I spent summer 2026 at the Computer Vision Lab at Warsaw University of Technology, funded by the ELIAS Mobility Fund, where we developed KANSteer, a method for steering diffusion transformers toward continuous visual concepts.

Prior to my doctoral studies, I received an MS in Computer Science from the National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), Islamabad, under the supervision of Dr. Faisal Shafait. My thesis addressed multilingual scene text understanding, specifically the detection and recognition of Urdu and English text on traffic signboards (TraffSign, DAS 2022). I also worked as a research assistant at the IHSAN Lab, Information Technology University (ITU), under Dr. Junaid Qadir, where my research covered adversarial machine learning, trustworthy AI, and scene understanding for autonomous driving, including R2S100K, a large-scale dataset for semi-supervised road-region segmentation (IJCV 2025).

selected publications

  1. Preprint
    Beyond the Linear Representation Hypothesis: Non-Linear Activation Steering in Text-to-Image Models
    Muhammad Atif Butt, Paweł Skierś, Joost van de Weijer, and Kamil Deja
    arXiv preprint, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Mechanistic interpretability often relies on the Linear Representation Hypothesis, which assumes that high-level concepts are encoded as linear directions in activation space. Yet natural visual concepts often pass through genuine intermediate states, and when text-to-image models are prompted for intermediate attributes, their activations rarely fall on the straight line connecting the endpoints. KANSteer models concept traversal as a curve through these intermediate states using Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, which provide a one-dimensional coordinate whose learned functions define the trajectory, so the steering direction varies along the concept while remaining interpretable. Across several concepts and text-to-image diffusion transformers, activation trajectories deviate substantially from straight lines, and KANSteer provides a closer fit and smoother traversal of intermediate attributes than linear steering.

  2. ECCV
    NumColor: Precise Numeric Color Control in Text-to-Image Generation
    Muhammad Atif Butt, Diego Hernández, Alexandra Gomez-Villa, Kai Wang, and 2 more authors
    In European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib Code Website

    Text-to-image diffusion models struggle with numerical color specifications such as hex codes and RGB values, because subword tokenization splits color codes into fragments the text encoder cannot map to consistent colors. NumColor introduces a Color Token Aggregator that detects color specifications regardless of tokenization and a ColorBook of 6,707 learnable embeddings aligned with CIE Lab space, together with auxiliary losses that keep the Lab and embedding geometries consistent. Trained on NumColor-Data, a synthetic set of 500K rendered images with exact color-to-pixel correspondence, the model is trained only on FLUX and transfers zero-shot to SD3, SD3.5, and PixArt models.

  3. CVPR
    GenColorBench: A Color Evaluation Benchmark for Text-to-Image Generation Models
    Muhammad Atif Butt, Alexandra Gomez-Villa, Tao Wu, Javier Vazquez-Corral, and 2 more authors
    In IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Code Website

    Recent text-to-image models still struggle with fine-grained color controllability and often fail to match the colors specified in prompts. GenColorBench is a benchmark of about 44K color-focused prompts covering more than 400 colors, grounded in color systems such as ISCC-NBS and CSS3/X11 and including numerical colors. It evaluates five tasks (color name understanding, multi-color composition, color-object association, numeric color understanding, and implicit color association) by combining VQA with perceptual color metrics.

  4. CVPR-W
    LumiCtrl: Learning Illuminant Prompts for Lighting Control in Personalized Text-to-Image Models
    Muhammad Atif Butt, Kai Wang, Javier Vazquez-Corral, and Joost van de Weijer
    In IEEE/CVF CVPR Workshop on Computer Vision for Editing (CVEU), 2026
    Oral Abs arXiv Bib HTML Website

    Oral

    Text-to-image models lack precise control over illumination. LumiCtrl learns an illuminant prompt from a single image of an object using three components: physics-based illuminant augmentation along the Planckian locus, edge-guided prompt disentanglement with a frozen ControlNet so that the prompt captures light rather than structure, and a masked reconstruction loss that focuses learning on the foreground while letting the background adapt.

  5. ECCV
    ColorPeel: Color Prompt Learning with Diffusion Models via Color and Shape Disentanglement
    Muhammad Atif Butt, Kai Wang, Javier Vazquez-Corral, and Joost van de Weijer
    In European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML Code Website

    Text-to-image models accept color names, but names cover wide ranges, so matching an exact color is hard, and existing personalization methods entangle color with object shape. ColorPeel generates simple 2D and 3D geometric shapes in the target color and learns a color prompt from them, explicitly disentangling color from shape so that the learned token carries color alone. The approach extends to abstract attributes such as textures and materials.
